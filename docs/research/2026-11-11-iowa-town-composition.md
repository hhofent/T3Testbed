## 1. What a small Iowa town contains

### 1.1 The commercial strip (Main Street = the highway)

Iowa towns have no separate downtown. The state highway **is** Main Street, and the
commercial strip is a single row of buildings along it. The reference towns (George,
Boyden, Hull) all show this: the E–W highway along the town's south edge, storefronts
facing it, residential grid behind.

Iowa PBS's "Businesses on Main Street" gives the classic progression:

| Town size | Businesses |
|---|---|
| 20–30 people | general store, post office, blacksmith shop, depot, lumberyard |
| prospering | hardware store, saloon, barbershop, pharmacy, millinery, dressmaker |
| established | banks, lumber yards, newspaper offices, churches on main street |
| larger | several of each; one or more newspapers; teachers, doctors, dentists, lawyers live in town |

**1990s-specific anchors.** By 1990 Casey's General Store was the single most common
Iowa small-town business: 983 stores nationally, **312 in Iowa**, and by 1996 the
third-largest convenience chain in the country. Its stated strategy was deliberate
concentration in small towns "where there is little competition" — 7-Eleven and Circle K
had abandoned them. Casey's in 1990 was a freestanding small-town store doing ~$800,000
of business, not a strip-mall unit. So *every* Iowa small town of any size had one.

A real 1990s Iowa small-town commercial row, in the order they usually appear:

- **Casey's General Store** — gas + grocery, often with the store's name in big letters
- **Farm implement dealership** — the biggest building on the strip; a big shed with a
  lot, "CALTRIDER MACHINERY" style (Casey, IA)
- **Feed & seed / co-op** — "FEED & SEED", "Farmers Feed & Supply", "Heartland CO-OP"
- **Grain elevator + bin row** — co-op owned, "Farmers Co-Op" signage on the elevator
- **Hardware store** — "HARDWARE"
- **Bank** — "CASEY MUTUAL TELCO"/bank on Main; one per town
- **Insurance agency** — a one-room office above or beside the bank
- **Post office** — federal building, often a distinct small block
- **Bar / grill / tavern** — "TIN LIZZIE'S SPORTS BAR", "BAR & GRILL"
- **Auto repair / electric / gas station** — "AUTO ELECTRIC", "CAR WASH AND STORAGE"
- **Pharmacy / drug store**, **restaurant**, **salon**, **lumber yard**
- Small service businesses: propane bulk tank, telco/phone co-op, insurance

### 1.2 Industrial — and industrial follows the *railroad*, not the highway

This is the single biggest gap in the generator. Evidence:

- Iowa DNR registers **hundreds of grain elevators** statewide; the vast majority are
  co-ops ("Farmers Elevator Cooperative", "Heartland CO-OP", "Rex Feed & Elevator").
  The co-op sign is painted on the elevator headhouse ("Sign for Farmers Co-Op" is
  literally a photo caption from the Iowa Heritage railroadiana collection).
- Elevators need a **rail siding**. The Bucknam, Iowa station was built by the railroad
  with "a railway siding was installed and a grain elevator built … to provide a country
  elevator market". The elevator is a railroad institution.
- In every reference town the elevator sits **at the rail/main junction**: Boyden's grain
  elevator silos at Railroad St & Main (west-centre); George's at the rail corridor.
- The NRHP industrial forms show the pattern for real factories: "Situated directly
  north of the former Chicago, Burlington and Quincy Railroad tracks"; Cedar Rapids had
  "industrial corridors extending along more than 40 miles of railroad right-of-ways";
  Vinton's industrial park; Iowa Malleable Iron in Fairfield.
- Small manufacturing starts as a **blacksmith shop and grows**: New Hampton Metal
  Fabrication began 1945 in a 20'×40' wooden shed; by 1989 a 60'×120' building. Ideal
  Industries in Vinton began as a metal fabrication shop. George's Boiler Ave is lined
  with "custom cabinet shops, Siebring Mfg, Oehmsen-Mid-West" — small manufacturing on
  the rail corridor.
- Industrial building form (Iowa Malleable Iron, NRHP): 1-story metal building on a
  reinforced concrete foundation, e.g. 90'×240' built 1991; concrete-block ancillary
  with 14–16 ft walls and overhead doors.

**What a small Iowa town had for industry in the 1990s:**

| Kind | Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Grain elevator + bin row | tall headhouse + concrete/steel bins, on a rail siding | the strongest Iowa marker; co-op owned |
| Feed mill / feed plant | long low building + bin row, often attached to the elevator | "CO-OP PLANT", "FEED CO-OP" |
| Implement dealership | big open shed + display lot, on the highway fringe | Shady Grove: "a machinery dealership"; Boyden: "Van's Power Center" |
| Feed & seed / fertilizer building | concrete block, e.g. 40'×120' with a 10'×50' scale | Aspinwall co-op |
| Small manufacturing / cabinet shop | 40'×80'–120' metal building, rail-side | George's Boiler Ave |
| Bulk propane / oil distributor | tank farm behind a shed | tanks visible on town edges |
| Creamery | milk plant | **rare by the 1990s** — the last Iowa butter creamery was Potter's Siding (1995); treat as a ghost-town element |

**Sizing by town class.** A crossroads (2×2 blocks) is not going to have an elevator and
a factory. The research says the *number* of stores depends on the number of people in
and around town. So the mix must scale with town class, and the elevator must only
appear where the rail actually touches the town.

### 1.3 Civic and institutional

From the Lyon County calibration (already in the generator) plus the web:

- School (often a single consolidated K–12 school) — Hull has two Christian high schools
- Churches — 1–5; often both a Protestant and a Catholic church; sometimes with a
  graveyard adjacent (Ardon: "St. Malachy's Catholic Church and graveyard")
- Public library — Hull, Boyden, Casey all have one
- City park + ball field (Hull: Westside and Eastside parks, a ball field)
- Cemetery — Hope Cemetery (Hull, NW)
- **Water tower** — a near-universal small-town landmark (Coulter, IA photo: "Water
  towers")
- **Railroad depot** — wooden, single story, small, with the town name sign on a post
  (Thornton, Bagley, Valeria, Coulter, Ardon all have depot photos)
- Hospital / nursing home in the bigger ones

### 1.4 Streets that shouldn't all be the same

Iowa towns grew in layers and their plats show it. Real extra streets:

- **A diagonal rail-corridor street** — George: Boiler Ave on the old rail line.
  Boyden: Railroad St curving SE through the south third.
- **A curved street** — Hull has one curved residential street on the east side.
- **A bypassed old highway** — when US 30 got a bypass, the old route stayed as a
  quieter parallel street (Kirkwood Blvd = old US 30 in Lisbon; Rolesville's Main St
  after the US 401 bypass).
- **Sparse rural-residential on an outlying road** — Hull: "North side has sparse
  rural-residential along 310th St outside the grid."
- **Streets that simply stop** at the edge of the plat, in a field.

## 2. Sidewalks, driveways, paths and entrances (county rules — hard numbers)

This is the most actionable section. Iowa county secondary-road policies are specific
and consistent, and they tell us exactly what the surface geometry should be.

### 2.1 Driveway / field entrance construction

From Woodbury, Jones, Appanoose, Clarke, Mills and O'Brien county policies:

| Rule | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Top width, driveway / field entrance | **min 20 ft, max 40 ft** | Appanoose; Woodbury ("up to 40 feet in top width") |
| Angle to the road centreline | **as near to 90° as site allows** | Jones |
| Side slopes, gravel road | **3:1 or flatter** | Clarke; Jones |
| Side slopes, paved road | **6:1 or flatter** (8:1 dry fill) | Clarke; Woodbury |
| Sight distance, gravel road | **400 ft** | Jones; Appanoose |
| Sight distance, paved road | **600 ft** | Jones |
| Offset from an intersecting road | **100 ft on gravel, 250 ft on paved** | Appanoose; Jones |
| Field entrance surface | earth w/ vegetation, or **min 4 in of 1⅛" crushed rock**; aggregate pavement 12 in | Clarke; Davenport standard detail |
| Culvert | minimum 18 in pipe (Davenport), 15 in metal (O'Brien), sized for the 50-yr storm | |
| Two drives per farm | **one for the residence + one for the buildings/bins** | Woodbury |
| Field entrances per field | **up to two per 40-acre field** | Woodbury |
| County surfacing | county lays gravel on entrances along paved roads and on occupied building sites on gravel roads | O'Brien |

### 2.2 Sidewalks

- Main Street has concrete sidewalks by the 1990s (board walks before the 1910s; towns
  sold raffle tickets to pay for board walks — "the Young People's Society sold tickets
  to … raise money for new board sidewalks").
- **Sidewalk networks are incomplete and patchy**, even in real towns: "there are not
  sidewalks everywhere"; "we walk on the streets and not usually the sidewalks … because
  sidewalks are cracked and not good"; "no sidewalks along any of the streets that cross
  US" (St. Olaf).
- In the Lyon County reference towns: **sidewalks along the E–W streets** (George), and
  the streets were gravel/dirt with concrete walks on top.
- Iowa DOT's own finding in a comprehensive plan: "there is currently no safe and
  accessible system allowing pedestrian access to both sides of the city" — even
  incorporated towns cross a highway with no sidewalk.

So the generator's "sidewalks on both sides of every street" is tidier than reality, but
the user explicitly wants the walks that lead to the houses — which is the right call:
a house with a front door and no walk from the street reads as unfinished.

### 2.3 What the generator already has vs. what is missing

Already present in `SmallTown.cs` / `MapGenerator.cs`:

- Street-edge concrete sidewalks on both sides of every E–W and N–S grid street (4.5 ft)
- A 4-ft concrete walk from the sidewalk to each residential front door
- Concrete driveway from the street to the garage door (17 ft wide), or a gravel
  alley drive where the garage is alley-loaded
- Commercial band: storefronts with labels (`CASEY'S`, `FEED & SEED`, `CO-OP`,
  `HARDWARE`, `BAR & GRILL`, `AUTO ELECTRIC`), an elevator + bin row, an industrial
  building (`MERCO MFG`, `CO-OP PLANT`, `SIOUX STEEL`, `MERIDIAN MFG`)
- Rural farmstead: 12-ft gravel drive to the garage from the road, a 10-ft gravel lane
  to the machine shed, a 3.5-ft concrete walk to the front door

Measured gaps (verified against a rendered town, `out/town4k.svg`, scale 0.318 px/ft):

- **Front-door walks on the E–W faces do not touch the sidewalk.** The sidewalk is
  drawn 2.25 ft inside the street's edge line; the walk starts 4.5 ft past the lot's
  front line. Measured median gap between a walk's street end and the nearest sidewalk
  line: **6.75 ft**. The side-lot walks (which face a N–S street) are correct — gap 0.
- **Garage drives also stop short of the sidewalk.** `zStart` is 2 ft past the front
  line (E–W) and 1.5 ft (side lots). In real Iowa a town driveway *crosses* the
  sidewalk and runs to the kerb.
- **Rural lots have no dirt field entrances.** Iowa sections have one or two dirt
  entrances off the gravel road for the farm fields, and the farm's own gravel drive is
  a *separate* entrance from the field entrances.
- **No field entrances anywhere in the world** — every section line is a clean edge
  between two fields.
- The farm's own gravel drive is already correct on angle: it leaves the road at ~90°
  and starts inside the 66-ft ROW (z −58, with the road at z −66…0).

## 3. The gap list, in one place

**Commercial** — the mix is a fixed list of six labels reused identically in every town,
so every town in the world has the same shops in the same order.

**Industrial** — the generator places the elevator and the industrial building in the
highway commercial band, but in the world map the *rail line may be up to 9 miles away*,
so a town can have an elevator with no railroad. Real elevators are railroad
institutions sitting on a siding at the rail/main junction.

**Civic** — no water tower, no depot, no cemetery, no ball field, no library as a
distinct building, no post office, no bank, no insurance office.

**Streets** — every town street is a straight grid leg; no diagonal rail-corridor street,
no curved street, no bypassed old highway, no sparse rural-residential on an outlying
road.

**Surfaces** — no dirt field entrances, no dirt paths, town sidewalks don't reach side
lots, garage drives don't always start at the kerb.
